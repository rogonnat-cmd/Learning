## What is personal investing?

Personal investing means putting money you won't need for a while into assets, such as shares of companies or loans to governments, that are expected to grow faster than prices rise. The aim is to have more buying power later, for retirement, a house, freedom, or whatever matters to you. The catch is risk: growth isn't guaranteed and values go up and down. Most of the skill is managing that trade-off calmly and cheaply.

## What you'll be able to do

- Decide whether you're ready to invest, or should pay off debt and build savings first
- Explain risk, return, diversification, and why costs matter so much
- Compare index funds, ETFs, mutual funds, and individual stocks
- Know which accounts to use and in what order (US first, adaptable to your country)
- Write a one-page investment plan and stick to it when markets crash
- Spot scams, conflicts of interest, and hype

## The journey, and why it's in this order

1. **M0 Before you invest:** why invest, and whether you're ready
2. **M1 How investments work:** risk, asset classes, diversification, costs
3. **M2 Vehicles:** funds vs. stocks, and active vs. passive
4. **M3 Accounts and taxes:** where to hold your investments
5. **M4 Building your portfolio:** your plan, your mix, rebalancing, your first order
6. **M5 Behavior and protection:** biases, crashes, scams, speculation
7. **M6 Later stages:** withdrawals and deeper topics

The order is deliberate. Each step needs the one before it: you can't choose a fund well without understanding costs and risk, and you can't stick to a plan without understanding your own behavior.
