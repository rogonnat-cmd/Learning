# Personal Investing — Study Curriculum

## Curriculum contract

- **Curriculum ID:** `pi-2026`
- **Session ID:** `pi-2026-s1`
- **Learner:** a self-directed adult learner who asked to learn "everything about personal investing."
- **Language:** English
- **Starting point (assumed, correct me if wrong):** a complete beginner. You don't need any finance or math background beyond basic arithmetic.
- **Target capability:** by the end, you can write and keep a simple, low-cost, diversified investing plan that fits your goals, and you can tell sound products and advice from bad ones on your own.
- **Scope:** the money basics you need before investing, risk and return, asset classes, funds, costs, accounts and taxes, building and maintaining a portfolio, investor behavior, fraud, speculation, and the basics of withdrawing money later.
- **Not covered:** professional trading, picking individual stocks as a career, detailed tax filing, and advice about your own situation. **This is education, not personal financial advice.**
- **Jurisdiction:** almost everything here applies in any country. **Module 3 (accounts and taxes) is written for the US for now.** Tell me your country and I'll rewrite it for you.
- **Effort:** about 25 lessons of 20–40 minutes each. That's roughly 10–15 hours, or 4–6 weeks at 3 lessons a week.
- **Research sweep date:** 2026-11-19. Check tax limits and rules again each year.

## How to use this curriculum

This file is the map; lessons live in `lessons/`. Take the lessons in order, because each module builds on the one before it. At the end of each module you can ask for an optional short check. Only lessons with a link exist so far; the others are marked *planned* and get written when you reach them.

## Learning path

### Module `M0` — Before you invest
**Outcome:** you can explain why people invest at all and decide whether *you* are ready to invest yet.

- **[`M0-L1` Why invest? Inflation, compounding, and time](lessons/M0-L1-why-invest.md)** (~30 min)
  - Outcome: explain how inflation erodes cash, how compounding works, and why starting early matters more than investing a lot.
- `M0-L2` *(planned)* Are you ready? Emergency funds, high-interest debt, and the order of operations.
- `M0-L3` *(planned)* Goals and time horizons: matching money to *when* you'll need it.

**Review checkpoint:** an optional check covering M0-L1 to L3, plus your own "money order of operations" list.

### Module `M1` — How investments work
**Outcome:** you can explain risk vs. return and describe the main asset classes and what each one does in a portfolio.

- `M1-L1` *(planned)* Risk and return: volatility, drawdowns, and why higher expected return comes with higher risk.
- `M1-L2` *(planned)* Asset classes: stocks, bonds, cash, real estate, commodities, and crypto.
- `M1-L3` *(planned)* Diversification and asset allocation.
- `M1-L4` *(planned)* Costs: expense ratios, fees, spreads, and why 1% a year matters so much.

### Module `M2` — Investment vehicles
**Outcome:** you can compare individual stocks, mutual funds, index funds, and ETFs, and explain the evidence on active vs. passive investing.

- `M2-L1` *(planned)* Owning single companies vs. owning funds.
- `M2-L2` *(planned)* Mutual funds, index funds, and ETFs: how they work and how they differ.
- `M2-L3` *(planned)* Active vs. passive: what the SPIVA scorecards show (`src-spiva`).
- `M2-L4` *(planned)* All-in-one options: target-date funds and robo-advisors.

### Module `M3` — Accounts and taxes *(US version; tell me your country to adapt it)*
**Outcome:** you can choose which accounts to fund, and in what order.

- `M3-L1` *(planned)* Tax-advantaged accounts: 401(k)/403(b), traditional vs. Roth IRA, HSA, employer match (`src-irs`).
- `M3-L2` *(planned)* An order for funding your accounts.
- `M3-L3` *(planned)* Taxable brokerage accounts: capital gains, dividends, and tax-loss harvesting.

### Module `M4` — Building and running your portfolio
**Outcome:** you can write a one-page investment plan and carry it out.

- `M4-L1` *(planned)* Writing your investment policy statement (your personal rules).
- `M4-L2` *(planned)* Choosing your stock/bond mix.
- `M4-L3` *(planned)* Lump sum vs. dollar-cost averaging, and automating contributions.
- `M4-L4` *(planned)* Rebalancing.
- `M4-L5` *(planned)* Opening a brokerage account and placing your first order (market vs. limit orders).

### Module `M5` — Behavior and protection
**Outcome:** you can recognize the behavioral traps, scams, and conflicts of interest that cost investors the most.

- `M5-L1` *(planned)* Behavioral biases: loss aversion, recency, overconfidence, and chasing past performance.
- `M5-L2` *(planned)* A plan for market crashes.
- `M5-L3` *(planned)* Scams, red flags, and choosing an adviser (fiduciary, fee-only).
- `M5-L4` *(planned)* Speculation: options, leverage, crypto, and hype stocks, and how to size them if you use them at all.

### Module `M6` — Later stages and going deeper
**Outcome:** you understand the basics of turning savings into income later, plus a few advanced topics.

- `M6-L1` *(planned)* Withdrawals: the "4% rule" and its limits.
- `M6-L2` *(planned)* Going deeper on bonds, international stocks, and factor investing.
- `M6-L3` *(planned)* Beneficiaries and estate basics.

## Review and retention

Start each new module with a two-minute recall of the previous one. Revisit Modules 1 and 5 one week after finishing them, because those ideas matter most when markets are falling.

## Completion criteria

You've finished the course when you have:
1. written your own one-page investment plan (M4-L1),
2. explained, in your own words, why low cost, diversification, and time beat stock picking for most people, and
3. worked through a "the market just dropped 30%" scenario using your plan (M5-L2).

## Gaps and uncertainty

- Return figures in lessons are **illustrative assumptions, not forecasts.**
- Module 3 is US-specific until you tell me your country.
- The SPIVA 15-year figure for large-cap funds comes from a secondary report. It gets checked against the full report before M2-L3.
